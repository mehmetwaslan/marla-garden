Planning a Conservatory in London

Home/Journal/Planning a Conservatory in London

Before committing to a conservatory design, establish which permissions and approvals apply to the property. Planning permission and building regulations address different questions; one does not replace the other.

Conservatories and planning permission

Planning Portal treats a conservatory as an extension or addition to a house. A proposal may fall within permitted development when all the relevant limits and conditions are met. This is a property-specific assessment, rather than a general exemption for anything called a conservatory.

Check the property before choosing the footprint

The type of property, previous extensions and restrictions affecting the site can change what is possible. Listed buildings, conservation areas and any restrictions on permitted development need particular attention. Rules for a house cannot automatically be applied to a flat or maisonette.

Building regulations are a separate assessment

Whether building regulations apply depends on the proposed construction and its relationship to the existing house. Discuss the floor area, separation from the house, heating arrangements, glazing, structural openings and other relevant work with building control. Do not assume that planning approval establishes compliance with building regulations.

Design and approvals should develop together

A roof shape, a boundary condition or a change to the existing wall can affect more than the appearance. Establish the proposed footprint and connection to the house early, so the design discussion and the professional assessment refer to the same scheme.

Questions for your project team

Ask who will assess the planning position, prepare any applications or drawings, coordinate structural information and obtain required approvals. Confirm those responsibilities in the scope before proceeding. Discuss the need for a lawful development certificate with the appropriate adviser where relevant.

Check the current official guidance

Use Planning Portal’s conservatory guidance as a starting point, then confirm the position for the actual property with the local planning authority and building control. This guide introduces the decisions to discuss; it is not a determination that a particular scheme is lawful or exempt.
